INTERPRETATION (honest — the matrix says "parity," not "faster")
  - Single-user decode (tg, concurrency 1) is consistently ~1.10-1.11x FASTER, and FLAT across context
    depth from 0 to 100k tokens. Clean signal, attributable to the vLLM-version drift below.
  - Prefill (pp2048) is ~0.75x (SLOWER), and high-concurrency aggregate throughput diverges
    (tg128 c10 = 0.73x). Likely vLLM batching/scheduling differences across the version gap and/or
    KV-pressure at the recipe's gpu-mem-util 0.7 + 262k max-model-len; not attributed definitively.
  - Net across the full matrix: median 1.01x = PARITY. We do not claim "Rocky is faster" — the stack
    reproduces the published matrix at parity, faster on single-user decode, slower on prefill/high-conc.

CONFOUND (the one uncontrolled variable)
  spark-arena pins no vLLM version (spark-vllm-docker compiles latest at image-build time). This entry was
  submitted 2026-03-03; our image built 2026-06-10 (~3 months newer). That gap is the most plausible source
  of both the decode gain and the prefill/high-concurrency divergence. A fully-controlled A/B would pin the
  entry-date vLLM; the leaderboard itself does not, so cross-date comparisons are runtime-version-drifted.
